# Inspiration from the Civic Tech Field Guide for Council Receipts

One-line purpose: what comparable civic-tech projects build, and the concrete features Council Receipts
should steal — prioritising citizen benchmarking, journalist forensics, and AI-native legibility.
Date: 5 October 2026. Author: research pass, no production code changed.

Method: I queried the Civic Tech Field Guide's public REST API
(`https://civictech.guide/api/v1/`, no key) for the relevant categories (Budget explorers, Taxpayer
receipt, Government transparency, Watchdogging government, Procurement oversight, Public meetings,
Meeting finders, Algorithmic transparency, Parliamentary monitoring) plus ~20 keyword searches, then
visited first-party project sites. Caveats on dead/JS-only pages are collected at the end — I flag
anything I could not actually read rather than inventing it.

## Top 10 actionable ideas

### 1. Source-linked "ask the record" — answers that click through to the exact document line
**Inspired by:** Open Council Network (https://opencouncil.network/about/journalists) — its
"ask the meeting a question" returns a direct quote plus a one-click jump to the moment in the video
or the paragraph in the document; citymeetings.nyc (https://citymeetings.nyc/) ships "AI and human
oversight", granular chapters and same-day segments; OpenCouncil.gr (https://opencouncil.gr/) links
each speaker position back to the exact video timestamp and the official decision on the Greek
transparency portal.
**Why it fits:** Council Receipts already has a "Copy briefing for AI" button, per-council dossiers
and an MCP server. The missing piece is an in-browser answer that is *grounded and checkable*: extract
from `decisions.csv`/document text and return the exact decision row or document with a deep link.
**Effort:** M. **Improves:** AI-native + journalist forensics. Extend the existing `search_decisions`
MCP tool with citation payloads and render the same in the forensic terminal.

### 2. Per-council and per-topic alerts/subscriptions (email, RSS, and a static JSON feed)
**Inspired by:** Open Council Network weekly email digests per council
(https://opencouncil.network/councils/camden); DáilDex "Follow a TD" source-linked email alerts
(https://daildex.com/); OpenCouncil.gr free WhatsApp/SMS/email alerts when a place, topic or councillor
is discussed (https://opencouncil.gr/); Boston Council tl;dr subscribe
(https://www.council-tldr.com).
**Why it fits:** the standout flags and decision stream are inherently trigger-based. A static site can
ship an RSS/JSON feed per council generated by the build, plus a "watch this council" that emails when
a new P1 flag or housing/enforcement decision appears. No server needed for RSS/JSON.
**Effort:** M. **Improves:** citizen benchmarking + engagement (the current site is pull-only).

### 3. A "How we made this / accuracy & corrections" page with a public error loop
**Inspired by:** Open Council Network's dedicated AI-accuracy page — what is AI-generated, explicit
"hallucinations are possible", what to do, and a "Content accuracy report" inbox
(https://opencouncil.network/ai-accuracy); Boston Council tl;dr's persistent "Experimental site… all
content is AI-generated and may contain errors. Learn more and share your feedback"
(https://www.council-tldr.com); Local Minutes' RAG + citation disclaimer
(https://localminutes.org); Council of AI's public corrections ledger and "unmeasured is never rounded
to zero" rule (https://councilof.ai/).
**Why it fits:** Council Receipts has METHODOLOGY.md, but the *site* doesn't say which numbers are
official, which are derived, which are AI-tagged, or how to flag a mistake. This is cheap and it is
exactly the credibility layer a councillor or journalist checks first.
**Effort:** S. **Improves:** trust, all audiences. Ship a footer link + per-panel source/as-of stamp.

### 4. Provenance-first data cards: every figure carries its source, its as-of date, or an explicit "no data"
**Inspired by:** Rallybase — "every figure carries the date we read it and the source we read it from;
where we have no data, the page says so rather than filling the gap with an estimate"
(https://rallybase.org); GovParti — "public information without context is just a pile of PDFs", with
Sources & Coverage and Trust pages (https://govparti.org); NGScorecard — "every claim linked to its
source" plus a per-card "Report an issue" (https://ngscorecard.com).
**Why it fits:** the decile flags and spend shares are powerful but currently float free of their
source. A small `source:`/`as_of:` per metric (MHCLG RSX 2024-25, Contracts Finder, Ombudsman, etc.)
and a visible "not available for this authority" state prevents the worst failure mode — a confident
number nobody can check.
**Effort:** S/M. **Improves:** trust + citizen benchmarking.

### 5. Procurement red flags: supplier concentration, single-bidder, and retender-soon signals
**Inspired by:** Dozorro — ML that scans Ukrainian government procurement contracts for signs of
corruption (https://dozorro.org/, listing https://civictech.guide/projects/dozorro — 403 to my fetch);
CouncilLedger — 118M+ UK council transactions, payments over £500, contract registers, forward plans,
supplier intelligence, with "full provenance tracking back to original sources"
(https://councilledger.app); Budeshi — links budget and procurement data to the public services they
buy (https://www.budeshi.ng/ — JS-only, see caveats); Open Contracting Data Standard for a schema to
map onto (https://www.open-contracting.org/data-standard/).
**Why it fits:** the forensic terminal already extracts £ values and contracts. The high-value
journalist flags are relational: the same supplier winning across many councils, one-bid tenders, and
contracts expiring soon — this is a build-script job over Contracts Finder, not a new data source.
**Effort:** M/L. **Improves:** journalist forensics.

### 6. Normalisation toggles so spending is legible, not just plotted
**Inspired by:** K-Monitor's Understandable Budget — functional vs economic view, and
nominal / inflation-adjusted / GDP-share toggles across a multi-year timeseries, with an
installable open-source framework (https://kozertheto.k-monitor.hu, code
https://github.com/k-monitor/onkormanyzati-koltsegvetes-v2); OpenBudgets.eu's audience-split fiscal
toolbox (https://openbudgets.eu/).
**Why it fits:** Council Receipts uses gross expenditure and population-weighted averages but the user
can't switch lens. Adding "per resident", "% of net budget", and "real terms (inflation-adjusted)" —
plus a functional-vs-economic classification — makes the same data defensible to a finance officer.
**Effort:** S/M. **Improves:** citizen benchmarking + credibility.

### 7. A promise/delivery tracker layered on decisions and budget lines
**Inspired by:** NGScorecard tracks recurring commitments across administrations as
Kept/Partial/Broken/In Progress (https://ngscorecard.com); DáilDex and Councilmatic support following
and comparing over time (https://daildex.com/, https://www.councilmatic.org/); OCN's cross-council
trend search (https://opencouncil.network/about/journalists).
**Why it fits:** the roadmap already lists "decision momentum". The stronger framing is promises vs
delivery: a budget line, repeated decisions, or a flag that persists across years. It turns an
observatory into an accountability product.
**Effort:** M. **Improves:** citizen benchmarking + journalist forensics.

### 8. Postcode / place entry and "what's happening near you"
**Inspired by:** Open Council Network postcode lookup (https://opencouncil.network/); Rallybase
ZIP → district with "meetings this week" and a map (https://rallybase.org); OpenCouncil.gr maps
decisions about a specific street or neighbourhood (https://opencouncil.gr/); UK peers TheyWorkForYou
and WriteToThem use postcode as the primary action entry (https://www.theyworkforyou.com/,
https://www.writetothem.com/).
**Why it fits:** a citizen doesn't know their ONS code. A postcode → council (+ ward where available)
landing page is the single highest-leverage acquisition improvement, and it sets up ward-level pins for
planning/enforcement decisions.
**Effort:** S/M. **Improves:** citizen benchmarking.

### 9. Action handoff: pre-filled councillor contact alongside the FOI builder
**Inspired by:** Rallybase — every page ends with something to do: contact an official, file a public
records request, send a physical letter (https://rallybase.org); WriteToThem — free email to
councillors/MPs (https://www.writetothem.com/); Alfasl27 — a platform for exercising the right of
access to information (listings https://civictech.guide/projects/alfasl27ma); Make Our Government
Accountable — constituent-verified contact (https://makeourgovaccountable.com/).
**Why it fits:** the forensic terminal already generates FOI text. Adding "email your ward councillor"
deep links per council (or pre-filled copy) closes the loop from evidence to action, using existing
public contact data.
**Effort:** S/M. **Improves:** engagement.

### 10. Crowdsourced marking against a published rubric (for the flags), and an open agent surface
**Inspired by:** Council Climate Action Scorecards — Climate Emergency UK scored *all* UK councils
against a published rubric, with a mySociety crowdsourced marking tool
(https://councilclimatescorecards.uk/ listing, https://github.com/mysociety/ceuk-marking); the Civic
Tech Field Guide's own machine surface — `/llms.txt`, `/api/openapi.json`, and a hosted MCP server at
`https://civictech.guide/mcp` (https://civictech.guide/build); OpenCouncil.gr's MCP at
https://opencouncil.gr/mcp; Council of AI's `/llms.txt` and agent card (https://councilof.ai/connect/).
**Why it fits:** Council Receipts already has llms.txt and MCP. Publishing the *rubric* behind each
standout flag (thresholds, sources, review date) enables both credibility and voluntary public
correction; and versioning a documented OpenAPI/REST surface next to the MCP makes the data reusable.
**Effort:** S (rubric) / M (marking tool). **Improves:** trust + AI-native.

## Projects worth studying in depth

- **Open Council Network** — https://opencouncil.network/ — UK, 175–270+ councils, AI summaries of
  meetings/decisions. Closest comparator. Lesson: segment by audience (citizens / councils /
  journalists / business / researchers), ship source-linked weekly digests, and treat AI accuracy as a
  product page. Has an API (/api/docs).
- **CouncilLedger** — https://councilledger.app — UK procurement intelligence over 16 years of
  Transparency Code data, 118M+ transactions, payments >£500, contract registers, forward plans.
  Lesson: provenance from council websites, and highlighting *low-value* contracts that never reach
  Find a Tender.
- **OpenCouncil.gr** — https://opencouncil.gr/ — Greek municipal meetings made legible: human-verified
  transcripts within 48h, per-speaker positions linked to video timestamps, decisions pinned to a map,
  place/topic/councillor alerts. Lesson: human-in-the-loop keeps AI trustworthy; map pins beat tables;
  MCP + API from day one.
- **citymeetings.nyc** — https://citymeetings.nyc/ — turns 12-hour hearings into shareable 3-minute
  segments same-day, with "AI and human oversight". Lesson: granularity + shareability is the whole
  product; request coverage is a growth mechanic.
- **Boston Council tl;dr** — https://www.council-tldr.com — AI summaries of Boston City Council,
  navigable by issue / neighbourhood / councilperson / meeting / docket, with a persistent AI-error
  disclaimer and feedback link. Lesson: trust framing and multi-axis navigation.
- **DáilDex** — https://daildex.com/ — Irish parliamentary monitoring: source-linked email alerts,
  "Ask Dex" cited AI, a documented methodology page, and a data API. Lesson: a clean methodology page
  plus "follow a representative" is a repeatable civic-monitoring formula.
- **Local Minutes** — https://localminutes.org — RAG chatbot over millions of pages of Vermont
  municipal documents, with citations and an explicit LLM-error disclaimer. Lesson: RAG + citation,
  and naming the technical method in the open.
- **Council Data Project** — https://councildataproject.org/ (homepage is JS-only to my fetcher) —
  open-source pipeline: videos → transcripts → voting records → searchable site. Lesson: the
  transcript/vote/search stack architecture, reusable across authorities.
- **Councilmatic (DataMade)** — https://www.councilmatic.org/ — search/browse/subscribe/comment on
  city council bills and events; long-running open-source (github.com/datamade). Lesson: subscriptions
  and comments as engagement, not just viewing.
- **Tainan City Council Observation Station** — https://tcco.tnsprout.org/ — district → councillor →
  interpellation search → analyses, plus a LINE chatbot (https://chatbot.tnsprout.org/). Lesson:
  organising by *district and person*, with separate "read analysis" content.
- **Rallybase** — https://rallybase.org — US civic reference with hard provenance discipline and an
  action on every page (letter, public-records request, contact). Lesson: "where we have no data, say
  so", and no ranking/endorsement by design.
- **NGScorecard** — https://ngscorecard.com — tracks promises across 213 administrations with
  kept/partial/broken status, source links, recurring-commitment threads, multilingual UI, report-an-
  issue. Lesson: longitudinal commitment tracking is a strong accountability frame.
- **GovParti** — https://govparti.org — connects fragmented local/state/federal data with explicit
  Sources & Coverage and Trust pages; markets by audience. Lesson: "context, not a pile of PDFs" as
  positioning.
- **K-Monitor Understandable Budget** — https://kozertheto.k-monitor.hu — municipal budget
  visualisation: functional vs economic view; nominal / inflation-adjusted / GDP-share; installable
  framework. Lesson: normalisation toggles make a budget comprehensible.
- **OpenBudgets.eu** — https://openbudgets.eu/ — fiscal toolbox split by audience (government, data
  wrangler, journalist), including investigative-journalism tools. Lesson: package the same data
  differently per audience.
- **Dozorro** — https://dozorro.org/ (403 to my fetch; listing
  https://civictech.guide/projects/dozorro) — ML red-flagging of procurement for corruption. Lesson:
  the analytics that turn contracts into leads.
- **Analyzing GoC Contract Spending** — https://goc-spending.github.io/ — volunteers scraped
  thousands of departmental HTML pages into a historical dataset, with a documented methodology.
  Lesson: methodology and downloadable data build authority.
- **Council Climate Action Scorecards** — https://councilclimatescorecards.uk/ (returned 503 to my
  fetcher; listing https://civictech.guide/projects/council-climate-action-scorecards) — assessed all
  UK councils against a published rubric using a crowdsourced marking tool. Lesson: rubric +
  crowdsourced marking as a campaign hook.
- **Civic Tech Field Guide itself** — https://civictech.guide/build — the directory's own MCP server,
  REST API, OpenAPI spec and llms.txt. Lesson: the machine surface is a first-class product.

## Patterns to avoid / already saturated

- **Yet another generic budget sunburst/treemap.** Budget visualisers are a crowded 2011–2017 genre
  (OpenBudgets.eu is legacy; Visual Town Budget's domain now resolves to an unrelated casino site —
  see caveats). The 2026 bar is context, provenance and action, not a new sankey. Council Receipts'
  advantage is the talk-vs-spend framing — protect that, don't dilute it into a standard explorer.
- **Unsourced "Ask AI" chat.** Open Council Network and Local Minutes both explicitly warn about
  hallucinations; OCN's selling point is that every claim traces to the record. Do not ship a chat box
  that cannot cite a decision row or document.
- **Single-number league tables / "best vs worst council".** Rallybase deliberately avoids scores and
  rankings; Council of AI refuses to round "unmeasured" into a ranking. Council Receipts' own
  Simpson's-paradox note about single-tier vs district councils is exactly why raw rankings mislead —
  keep the tier toggle and frame flags as decile positions with caveats, not verdicts.
- **Scraping when an official file exists.** GoC-Spending shows the cost of HTML scraping; Council
  Receipts already prefers official files (RSX, Single Data List, Transparency Code). Keep it that
  way; use scraping only where there is no schema (e.g. council forward plans).
- **PDF-only or image-only outputs.** The dossiers + llms.txt are the correct direction; avoid
  "download the 200-page PDF" as the only artifact.
- **Promotional/vibecoded framing.** A chunk of new Field Guide entries are tagged "Vibecoded" with
  self-reported numbers. Treat impressive stats on commercial sites as claims, not facts, and cite them
  as such.
- **Ignoring movement/history.** The most useful comparators (NGScorecard, OCN, DáilDex, K-Monitor) all
  show time. A one-shot snapshot ages badly; the roadmap's momentum work is correctly prioritised. Do
  not ship a "static year" version as the final product.

## Research caveats

- The Field Guide's directory front-end (`https://app.civictech.guide/`) is a JS app; I read the same
  curated records through its public REST API (`https://civictech.guide/api/v1/projects/...`,
  `.../categories`, no key). Individual `civictech.guide/projects/<slug>` pages exist but some render
  client-side.
- Dead, blocked or JS-only pages encountered (stated rather than invented): `arlingtonvisualbudget.org`
  (Visual Town Budget) now redirects to a German-language online-casino site — treat that listing as
  dead; `dozorro.org` returned HTTP 403 to my fetch; `budeshi.ng` requires JavaScript; the Council Data
  Project and Bellingcat Council Meeting Transcript Search homepages are JS-only; `councilclimatescorecards.uk`
  returned 503 to my fetcher (HTTP 200 to curl, so probably bot-blocking).
- The directory skews non-UK. Only a handful of direct UK council comparators exist (Open Council
  Network, CouncilLedger, Open Council Data https://opencouncildata.co.uk/, Council Climate Action
  Scorecards); the rest are transferable patterns, not drop-in fitting.
- Most performance numbers quoted (118M transactions, 270+ councils, 7,000 agenda subjects, 16 years)
  are the projects' own self-reported figures, not independently verified here.
- No files other than this report were modified.
